February 14, 2024

Jamie McCourt — former Los Angeles Dodgers CEO and Trump fundraiser — donated $10,000 to GOP Guam.

McCourt's contribution was the first of 193 donations of exactly $10,000 that the Republican Party of Guam received in 2024.

The $10,000 figure wasn't random. It represented the annual individual contribution limit to a state party committee at the time.

And all 193 contributions came through Trump 47 Committee Inc.

A group tip jar.

Trump 47 Committee Inc. was a joint fundraising committee, or JFC.

Instead of a donor writing separate checks to several political committees, a JFC can accept one large contribution and allocate portions of it among participating committees.

Under Trump 47's allocation structure, portions first went to Trump's primary campaign, Trump's general-election campaign, Save America PAC and the Republican National Committee.

Only after those allocations could money move farther down the list to participating state and territorial party committees.

Why Guam?

Based on Trump 47's allocation structure, donors reaching state and territorial party accounts had already filled earlier allocations to Trump's campaign, Save America PAC and the RNC.

But receiving the money was only half the story.

The next reporting question

Where did the money go?

The FEC filings provide the paper trail.
The money didn't stay in Guam.
The filings show transfers from the territorial party to its affiliated national committee.
97.83%

of the money received by GOP Guam was subsequently transferred to the Republican National Committee, according to the original analysis of FEC filings.

Suspicions confirmed.

The finding wasn't simply that wealthy donors had contributed to a distant territorial party.

It was that nearly all of the money received by GOP Guam subsequently moved to the RNC.

Federal rules permit transfers between affiliated national, state and territorial party committees without the same individual contribution ceiling imposed when a person gives directly.

So why didn't these donors just give another $10,000 directly to the RNC?

Because the donors reaching GOP Guam through Trump 47 had already filled the RNC allocation that came earlier in the committee's distribution formula.

The bigger question.

Contribution limits are intended to restrict how much money one donor can give directly to a political committee.

But the Guam transactions illustrate the tension between individual contribution limits and rules allowing affiliated party committees to transfer funds among themselves.

Following the first donation isn't enough.

You have to follow where the money goes next.
